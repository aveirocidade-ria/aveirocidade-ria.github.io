WHY I AM WORRIED

I don't want us to notice what we've lost when it's already too late

I walk around Antiga Lota and the nearby salt pans, watch birds moving over the water, and find it hard to imagine this place with more roads, buildings and traffic. Yet that is the future being planned for the old port land.

In October 2025, I learned that Aveiro's municipality had commissioned the detailed project for Parque das Marinhas, worth €730,976. The plans include housing, a hotel and other urban uses. The announcement also mentioned geological and geotechnical investigations. It doesn't mean construction has started. But it does mean these plans are moving beyond drawings and ideas.

And this is what worries me most: I still don't know when work might begin, what has finally been approved, or what studies have been done on how the project could affect the birds and wetlands nearby. Perhaps some of those answers are already in official documents I haven't been able to see. But has the city really considered leaving this land free of new buildings and giving nature more room? I haven't found a clear answer to that either.

I don't want to wait until the only question left is how to make the construction less damaging. I want us to have the chance to ask whether it should happen here at all. If you love the Ria, its birds and its open spaces, I hope you'll add your voice while there is still time to discuss a different future.

Não quero esperar até que a única discussão possível seja como tornar a construção menos prejudicial. Quero que ainda possamos perguntar se faz sentido construir aqui. WHO WAS ASKED?

But did the people of Aveiro get to choose?

Imagine walking into an exhibition about the future of Antiga Lota. It is February 2023. On the walls are architects’ drawings of what this old waterfront might become. Visitors can look at the proposals and leave their comments.

But there is a question that comes before the drawings. What if we don't want a new neighbourhood here at all? What if we would rather keep this part of the Ria quieter, with its boats, open spaces and room for nature?

Being invited to comment on the design of new buildings is not quite the same as being invited to decide whether they should be built. I know that an exhibition took place and comments were welcomed. What I have not yet found in the records I reviewed is a wider public debate where keeping the area largely free of new housing was clearly put forward as an alternative.

These are not just my questions. In November 2025, the Aveiro branch of the environmental organisation Quercus also raised concerns about transparency and public participation. Before the future of this waterfront is decided, shouldn't people have a chance to discuss the choice itself, not only the appearance of what might be built?

ABOUT THE PROJECTSOBRE O PROJETO

This project is not about me

Aveiro Cidade–Ria explores how a growing city lives alongside the Ria de Aveiro and the natural world around it. Antiga Lota is the first investigation: a place where plans for new buildings raise questions about wildlife, public space and who gets to decide.

It began with one resident paying attention to the Ria de Aveiro. I am not an ecologist, and I do not speak for an organisation. This is an independent project, written from the perspective of someone who lives near this landscape and cares about what happens to it.

I understand why cities need homes, services and public funds. But that does not mean every available piece of land should be built over. Nature has the right to exist without needing human permission. Where people have already changed a place, we can sometimes help it recover instead of filling it with more buildings.

This project is for everyone who loves the Ria and believes that its living, breathing landscape deserves a future of its own. People can be neighbours, careful visitors and guardians — not always the ones who decide that another piece of nature needs to be conquered.

Este projeto não é sobre mim

Aveiro Cidade–Ria explora a relação entre uma cidade em crescimento, a Ria de Aveiro e a natureza à sua volta. A Antiga Lota é a primeira investigação: um lugar onde os planos para novos edifícios levantam questões sobre a vida selvagem, o espaço público e quem pode decidir.

Começou com uma pessoa que vive perto da Ria de Aveiro e presta atenção ao que acontece à sua volta. Não sou ecologista nem represento uma organização. Este é um projeto independente, feito por alguém que gosta deste lugar e se preocupa com o seu futuro.

Habituámo-nos a olhar para um terreno e perguntar o que podemos construir nele, quanto dinheiro pode render ou no que se poderá transformar. Acho que está na altura de fazer outra pergunta. A terra não está vazia. Está cheia de vida. As aves passam por ali. As plantas crescem. A água move-se. Tudo faz parte de um sistema vivo, quer dê lucro, quer não.

Compreendo que as cidades precisem de habitação, serviços e receitas. Mas isso não significa que todos os terrenos disponíveis tenham de ser construídos. A natureza tem o direito de existir sem precisar da autorização humana. Onde as pessoas já alteraram um lugar, por vezes podemos ajudá-lo a recuperar, em vez de o encher com mais edifícios.

Este projeto é para todas as pessoas que gostam da Ria e acreditam que esta paisagem viva, que respira, merece ter um futuro próprio. Podemos viver ao lado da natureza, observá-la e cuidar dela — sem achar que temos sempre de conquistar mais um pedaço.

A PLACE TO HEAR THE BIRDS

Where can I find a little quiet in the centre of Aveiro?

I walk around central Aveiro almost every day. Along some of the canals, cars and buses are never far away. On the waterfront paths towards the A25, motorway traffic dominates the sound. Even where the road is smaller, near the access routes and junctions, the engines can drown out birdsong. Today I tried to identify a bird by its call, but the passing cars made it difficult to hear.

That is why the pedestrian Rua do Sal and the quieter surroundings of Antiga Lota matter so much to me. Of the places I regularly walk near the centre, these are among the very few where I can slow down and actually listen to the water and the birds. I am not claiming that they are objectively the only quiet places in all of Aveiro. I am describing something missing from my everyday experience of the city.

Now imagine years of construction here, followed by new buildings, deliveries, cars and a much busier waterfront. What happens to the possibility of simply being somewhere quiet? The precise effects still need assessment, but the risk of losing this experience deserves to be part of the decision — not an afterthought once the development is under way.

Before the city decides to build, shouldn't it ask what people would lose if this place stopped being quiet? This is not only about preserving a view. It is about access to nature, rest and an everyday public space that doesn't ask us to buy anything.

And there is a legal reason to take this seriously. Portuguese noise law does not treat a pleasant acoustic environment as an irrelevant luxury. Article 6 of Decree-Law 9/2007 says municipal land-use plans must safeguard sound quality, taking existing and expected sources into account. Decree-Law 146/2006 also recognises the preservation of quieter areas in noise management. Aveiro's own municipal plan (PDM) publishes a map classifying sensitive and mixed noise zones (sheet 1.16).

So I want to know what these documents say about this exact waterfront: how is it classified today, what noise is already present, and what change is expected from construction, new roads and daily activity? Was a less intensive alternative — one that restored the shoreline while preserving quiet public access — actually assessed?

I have not found a publicly accessible project-specific acoustic assessment or a documented comparison with a no-new-buildings alternative in the sources checked so far. That does not prove such documents do not exist. Nor does it mean Antiga Lota is an officially designated zona tranquila. It means these are unresolved questions worth examining before the decision becomes irreversible.

These are personal observations, not a formal acoustic survey or a legal designation of a protected “quiet area”. I want municipal acoustic maps and the project's assessment to clarify the current situation and possible future impacts. Aveiro PDM and municipal noise maps ↗

UM LUGAR ONDE POSSO OUVIR AS AVES

Onde posso encontrar um pouco de sossego no centro de Aveiro?

Passeio pelo centro de Aveiro quase todos os dias. Junto de alguns canais, os carros e os autocarros nunca estão muito longe. Nos percursos junto à água, em direção à A25, o trânsito da autoestrada domina o som. Mesmo junto às estradas mais pequenas e aos acessos, os motores podem abafar o canto das aves. Hoje tentei identificar uma ave pelo seu canto, mas os carros que passavam dificultaram muito a escuta.

É por isso que a Rua do Sal, agora pedonal, e os espaços mais tranquilos à volta da Antiga Lota são tão importantes para mim. Entre os lugares por onde costumo passear perto do centro, são dos poucos onde consigo abrandar e ouvir realmente a água e as aves. Não estou a afirmar que sejam os únicos lugares tranquilos de toda a cidade. Estou a falar daquilo que sinto faltar na minha experiência quotidiana de Aveiro.

Agora imaginem anos de obras neste lugar e, depois, novos edifícios, entregas, carros e uma frente de água muito mais movimentada. O que acontece à possibilidade de simplesmente estar num lugar sossegado? Os efeitos exatos ainda precisam de avaliação, mas o risco de perder esta experiência merece entrar na decisão — não ser lembrado quando a obra já estiver a decorrer.

Antes de decidir construir, a cidade não deveria perguntar o que as pessoas perdem se este lugar deixar de ser tranquilo? Não se trata apenas de preservar uma vista. Trata-se de ter acesso à natureza, ao descanso e a um espaço público onde não é preciso consumir nada.

E há também uma razão legal para levar esta questão a sério. A legislação portuguesa sobre o ruído não trata a qualidade do ambiente sonoro como um luxo irrelevante. O artigo 6.º do Decreto-Lei n.º 9/2007 determina que os planos municipais assegurem a qualidade do ambiente sonoro, considerando as fontes de ruído existentes e previstas. O Decreto-Lei n.º 146/2006 também contempla a preservação de zonas tranquilas na gestão do ruído. O próprio PDM de Aveiro publica um mapa de classificação das zonas sensíveis e mistas (folha 1.16).

BIRDS AT DUSK

Where do the birds go after sunset?

Around sunset, something happens above Antiga Lota. I'm out walking, the light is fading, and suddenly I notice birds crossing the sky — ducks, gulls and other flocks. Some pass right over the old port land, others along its edges, on their way across the lagoon. I see it again and again. I don't know whether each flock is migrating or simply travelling between feeding and resting places. Either way, this space is part of their journey.

Now imagine a hotel and homes here: bright windows, outdoor lights, glass and more noise, where today the birds fly through the evening air. Would their routes change? Could the lights affect them? We shouldn't have to guess after everything is built.

There are good reasons to ask now. A study in Portugal's Tagus estuary found that lighting changed how some shorebirds used habitat and fed at night. A radar study on the Croatian coast linked artificial light to changes in nocturnal migrants' flight altitude and speed. And a review of 36 studies found effects of night lighting on birds' behaviour and physiology. Effects differ by species; none of these papers tells us exactly what would happen at Antiga Lota.

That's why I want an independent survey of the birds' evening flights — which species pass here, when, in which direction and at what height — before decisions about building height, glass and lighting become irreversible.

The land isn't empty. The water isn't empty. And neither is the sky.

AVES AO ANOITECER

Para onde vão as aves depois do pôr do sol?

À hora do pôr do sol, acontece uma coisa sobre a Antiga Lota. Estou a passear, a luz começa a desaparecer e, de repente, reparo nas aves a atravessar o céu — patos, gaivotas e outros bandos. Alguns passam mesmo por cima dos terrenos do antigo porto; outros seguem pelos lados, atravessando a paisagem da Ria. Vejo isto muitas vezes. Não sei se cada bando está em migração ou se simplesmente se desloca entre locais de alimentação e descanso. Mas, de uma forma ou de outra, este espaço faz parte do seu percurso.

Agora imaginem aqui um hotel e habitações: janelas iluminadas, luzes exteriores, vidro e mais ruído, precisamente onde hoje as aves cruzam o céu ao entardecer. Será que mudariam de rota? A iluminação poderia afetá-las? Não devíamos deixar estas perguntas para depois da construção.

Há razões científicas para as fazer já. Um estudo no estuário do Tejo mostrou alterações na utilização do habitat e na alimentação noturna de algumas aves limícolas devido à iluminação. Um estudo com radar na costa da Croácia associou a luz artificial a alterações na altitude e na velocidade de voo de aves migradoras noturnas. E uma revisão de 36 estudos encontrou efeitos da iluminação noturna no comportamento e na fisiologia das aves. Os efeitos variam consoante a espécie; nenhum destes trabalhos permite prever exatamente o que aconteceria na Antiga Lota.

É por isso que peço um estudo independente dos voos das aves ao entardecer — que espécies passam, a que horas, em que direção e a que altura — antes de as decisões sobre altura dos edifícios, vidros e iluminação se tornarem irreversíveis.

A terra não está vazia. A água não está vazia. E o céu também não.

A QUESTION OF PUBLIC VALUE

Who will enjoy the lagoon — and who will pay for the changes?

In November 2023 the municipality itself acknowledged that high tides flooded much of Antiga Lota and that raising surrounding walls before urbanisation would require substantial extra investment. So this is not a plot of land that can simply be built on without additional work.

The municipality's October 2025 announcement describes a planned neighbourhood with housing, a hotel and commercial uses, as well as a public park and nautical facilities. It awarded €730,976 for the detailed development project; this is a design contract, not the price of flood protection or construction. A 2026 geotechnical study procurement followed.

Waterfront apartments with lagoon views could be expensive. But no verified sales prices or binding affordable-housing allocation for this specific development have been established in the documents reviewed here. Nor do we yet know who will ultimately fund flood defences and how those costs will be distributed. Public spending on protection does not automatically mean the entire cost is added to apartment prices.

My question is about the choice itself: if keeping this land buildable requires expensive work, why is private residential development the preferred future? Why not compare its full public cost with repairing the shoreline and keeping a quiet, open place for everyone? The lagoon, the view and the silence are public riches. They should not become privileges available mainly to those able to buy the most expensive homes.

O anúncio municipal de outubro de 2025 prevê habitação, hotel, comércio, parque verde e instalações náuticas. Mas, nos documentos analisados, ainda não encontrei preços de venda confirmados nem uma quota vinculativa de habitação acessível para este projeto específico. Também não está esclarecido quem pagará as obras de proteção e como serão repartidos esses custos. O investimento público em proteção não significa automaticamente que todo o valor seja incorporado no preço das casas.

Para mim, a verdadeira questão é a escolha: se é necessário um investimento elevado para tornar esta área urbanizável, por que razão se privilegia a construção residencial privada? Por que não comparar o custo público total da urbanização com a recuperação da margem e a preservação de um lugar aberto e tranquilo para todos? A Ria, a paisagem e o sossego são riquezas comuns. Não deveriam tornar-se privilégios reservados sobretudo a quem pode pagar as casas mais caras.

The lagoon, long before the plans

The Ria de Aveiro did not begin as vacant land. Its channels and saltmarshes took shape over centuries through moving sand, rivers, tides and human activity. The coastal barrier developed from around the tenth century; in 1808, the present inlet was opened to the sea.

Fishing, salt production and navigation are part of this landscape's human history. But the history of the whole lagoon is not the same as the history of the Antiga Lota parcel. The latter includes former port and fish-market structures, and parts of the land have been altered by people. The exact history of individual plots is still being researched.

NATURE AND LAW

The Ria is alive — and its nature is protected

Look across the Ria de Aveiro and you will see much more than water. There are mudflats, saltmarsh plants, fish and birds. Some birds stop here to rest or feed during their journeys; others live and breed around the lagoon. These places are connected. Changes in one part of the shoreline can affect life beyond it.

Parts of the Ria are protected by Natura 2000, a European network of places important for nature. Portugal approved a new conservation plan for these areas in 2026. That means the effects of nearby development also matter: a project does not have to stand inside a protected area to affect it.

For Antiga Lota, the question is straightforward: before adding more buildings, traffic, noise and lighting, have the effects on nearby habitats and birds been properly studied? The precise boundaries and findings should be available for everyone to examine.

WHAT HAPPENS DURING THE WORKS?

Years of construction — what happens to the Ria in the meantime?

The old port strip is not abandoned. People walk here, boats moor here, and nautical clubs use the waterfront. Nearby reeds, water and salt pans are part of the wider lagoon landscape. The proposed neighbourhood would not arrive overnight: construction could mean lorries, machinery, dust, noise, brighter lights and temporary barriers beside the water.

These are potential effects, not a confirmed construction timetable. I have not yet found a publicly verified start date, duration, phasing plan, or clear description of which paths, shorelines and nautical activities would remain accessible during the works.

The concern is not only what the neighbourhood will look like when it is finished, but what happens during the works. Construction near walking paths and nautical facilities could make this currently accessible waterfront louder, busier and less pleasant to visit. Birds may avoid areas exposed to repeated disturbance; whether that happens here, and which species or habitats would be affected, needs a site-specific assessment rather than an assumption that all wildlife will disappear.

What would be done to protect birds and other wildlife? How would dust, stormwater runoff, noise and night-time lighting be controlled? Could some existing buildings be restored without turning this waterfront into a new urban district? These questions matter just as much as the finished architectural drawings.

OTHER CHOICES WERE POSSIBLE

When a waterfront was not turned into another development

A plan can be approved, money can be spent, and still a town may choose a different future for its shore. These places are not identical to Antiga Lota. But they show that building is not the only possible answer — and that changing direction has practical and financial consequences.

OUTRAS ESCOLHAS FORAM POSSÍVEIS

Quando uma frente de água não se tornou mais uma urbanização

Um plano pode ser aprovado, pode já ter havido despesas e, ainda assim, uma comunidade pode escolher outro futuro para a sua margem. Estes lugares não são iguais à Antiga Lota. Mas mostram que construir não é a única resposta — e que mudar de rumo também tem consequências práticas e financeiras.

CATALONIA · SPAIN

Aiguamolls de l’Empordà — a development that was stopped

In 1974, the Port Llevant marina plan proposed canals and housing across 525 hectares of coastal wetlands, with capacity for 65,000 people. The plan was not just a sketch: work began on an access road in 1977, but the authorities ordered it stopped because permission was missing for work in a protected scenic area. A later government study supported protection, and the development plan was invalidated. In 1983, Catalonia passed a law protecting the wetlands.

What about the money already spent? The developer had already started work. Research also records that the Catalan government bought a building owned by Port Llevant in 1984. However, the sources reviewed do not establish the full amount invested, any total compensation or the project's complete financial settlement. I cannot claim that reversing the development was cost-free.

The lesson for Aveiro: a previously approved urban plan and some construction were not the end of the discussion. But Aveiro's land-transfer agreement and financial obligations would need their own legal and budget analysis.

CATALUNHA · ESPANHA

Aiguamolls de l’Empordà — uma urbanização que foi travada

Em 1974, o projeto da marina residencial Port Llevant previa canais e habitação em 525 hectares de zonas húmidas costeiras, com capacidade para 65 000 pessoas. Não era apenas um desenho: em 1977 começaram trabalhos num acesso, que foram mandados parar pelas autoridades por falta de autorização numa área de interesse paisagístico. Um estudo posterior do governo regional defendeu a proteção e o plano urbanístico foi anulado. Em 1983, a Catalunha aprovou uma lei de proteção das zonas húmidas.

E o dinheiro que já tinha sido gasto? O promotor já tinha começado os trabalhos. A investigação também regista que, em 1984, o governo catalão comprou um edifício pertencente à Port Llevant. Mas as fontes consultadas não permitem confirmar o investimento total, eventuais indemnizações ou o custo final da mudança. Não podemos afirmar que travar o projeto não teve custos.

Para Aveiro: nem um plano aprovado nem trabalhos já iniciados encerraram o debate. Mas o acordo de transferência dos terrenos e as obrigações financeiras de Aveiro exigem uma análise própria.

L’ESTARTIT · SPAIN

La Pletera — undoing an unfinished neighbourhood

Housing had been planned on coastal marshland, and part of the urban infrastructure was built. The scheme stalled, the land was reclassified as non-developable in 2002, and a later restoration project removed abandoned urban works and helped recover lagoons and dunes. The LIFE Pletera project had a budget of about €2.53 million, 75% funded by the European Union.

The lesson: even land that has already been altered by roads and building works can be restored. This is not proof that restoration at Antiga Lota would be technically possible everywhere — that needs local studies.

L’ESTARTIT · ESPANHA

La Pletera — desfazer uma urbanização inacabada

Estava prevista habitação numa zona húmida costeira e parte das infraestruturas chegou a ser construída. O projeto parou, o terreno passou a ser não urbanizável em 2002 e, mais tarde, uma intervenção retirou obras urbanas abandonadas e ajudou a recuperar lagoas e dunas. O projeto LIFE Pletera teve um orçamento de cerca de 2,53 milhões de euros, 75% financiados pela União Europeia.

A lição: mesmo terrenos já alterados por obras podem ser recuperados. Isto não prova que a recuperação de toda a Antiga Lota seja tecnicamente possível — seriam necessários estudos locais.

CALDAS DA RAINHA · PORTUGAL

Paul de Tornada — a town protects a wetland

After years of work by the local environmental association PATO and others, Caldas da Rainha established a local nature reserve in 2009. This was not the cancellation of an equivalent hotel project; it is an example of municipal leadership in protecting reeds and wetland habitats rather than treating them only as development land.

CALDAS DA RAINHA · PORTUGAL

Paul de Tornada — um município protege uma zona húmida

Após anos de trabalho da associação ambiental PATO e de outros intervenientes, as Caldas da Rainha criaram uma reserva natural local em 2009. Não foi o cancelamento de um projeto hoteleiro equivalente; é um exemplo do papel de um município na proteção de caniçais e zonas húmidas, em vez de considerar estes terrenos apenas como espaço para construir.

ALCOCHETE · PORTUGAL

Salinas do Samouco — paying to keep salt pans alive

As part of environmental commitments linked to the Vasco da Gama Bridge, around 300 hectares of salt pans were expropriated from 1997 onward and restoration began. Here, funding and land management were organised to preserve an existing coastal habitat. It was a mitigation obligation, not a community victory over a hotel scheme.

ALCOCHETE · PORTUGAL

Salinas do Samouco — investir na conservação das salinas

No âmbito dos compromissos ambientais ligados à Ponte Vasco da Gama, cerca de 300 hectares de salinas foram expropriados a partir de 1997 e foram iniciados trabalhos de recuperação. Neste caso, organizaram-se financiamento e gestão do terreno para preservar um habitat costeiro existente. Tratou-se de uma obrigação de compensação ambiental, não de uma vitória popular contra um hotel.

The question for Aveiro is not whether stopping a project costs money. It may. It is whether the public has been shown the full cost of building, the full cost of changing course, and what would be gained by protecting the lagoon landscape instead.
A questão para Aveiro não é saber se travar um projeto tem custos. Pode ter. É saber se foram apresentados ao público os custos totais de construir, os custos de mudar de rumo e os benefícios de proteger a paisagem lagunar.
